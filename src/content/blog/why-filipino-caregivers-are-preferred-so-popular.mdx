When one thinks of the Philippines, migrant workers immediately spring to mind. **Over 10 million Filipinos are working in more than 200 countries.** In other words, nearly 10 percent of the population is living abroad.

They are scattered all over the world. Some are toiling on construction sites in the Gulf, some are caring for patients in hospitals across Europe, and others are providing domestic services in East Asia. But they all share the same concern: sending the bulk of whatever they earn back to their families at home. These remittances have become so vital that they now form the backbone of the Philippine economy. The government is well aware of this, which is why it has given them the name **"Bagong Bayani" — "New Heroes."** They know these are the people on whom the country truly relies.

The most striking aspect is just how prominent women are in this sector. Filipino women, particularly in the care and service sectors, have now become something of an international brand. So what is the secret behind their success? There are two main reasons. Firstly, historically, the level of education amongst women in the Philippines has already been quite high, and English is very widespread in everyday life. But that alone does not explain everything. What really makes the difference is the labor recruitment system the government has been establishing for many years. The system is so well-established that going abroad is no longer just a matter of "taking a chance." There is a significant preparation process involved.

They undergo specialized training tailored to the country they are going to. They don't just teach the language; they also explain that country's employment regulations, workers' rights, and even what to do if something goes wrong. This training can sometimes last for months. That's why, when an employer chooses a Filipino female worker, they aren't just taking on an employee; they're taking on someone who's been thoroughly prepared and well-informed from start to finish. That's precisely why they're in such high demand. The system really does work very effectively.



## Why are Filipino women in such high demand?

When it comes to the high demand for Filipino women abroad, particularly in the domestic or care sectors, one should not look solely for economic reasons. The issue runs much deeper than that. There are distinct characteristics that set them apart from other alternatives: their fluency in English, high levels of education, exceptional work discipline, and, in fact, the image of the **"reliable worker"** that the Philippine government has been carefully cultivating for years.

### Language advantage (Advanced English)

Undoubtedly, the most important characteristic of Filipino workers is their command of English. Families want their children to be exposed to a foreign language from a young age and to learn English naturally. That is why Filipino carers are always their first choice. Then, of course, there is the financial aspect; compared to the astronomical fees demanded by American or British carers whose first language is English, Filipino carers are able to provide the same high-quality service at much more reasonable rates.

### An educated and skilled workforce

A large proportion of Filipino women working abroad are, in fact, **university graduates** or have received higher education. Many women who work as teachers, nurses, or business managers in their own country agree to work as domestic helpers abroad in order to earn a better income. This is a significant advantage for employers; they are not merely bringing in someone to clean the house but a highly educated, competent, and conscientious individual.

### Global Reputation: "Brand Philippines"

Filipino workers enjoy a well-established and highly respected reputation worldwide. This perception of the "Philippines Brand" did not arise by chance; it is the product of both historical processes and government policies. In the workplace, they are generally known for being **cooperative, loyal, dedicated, disciplined, and trouble-free.** They are truly hard-working. There is no need to stand over them and tell them what to do; they carry out their duties meticulously and always maintain a professional distance.

### Emotional labour and the culture of care

What sets Filipinos apart in the care sector is that they do not view the work merely as a duty. The genuine bond they form is quite unique. They show the children in their care the same compassion as they would their own children, and they treat the homes where they work as if they were their own. Their sense of protecting and looking after the family is so strong that this sensitivity is virtually held in the highest regard by employers.

### Social Status and Prestige

In some countries and among high-income families, employing a Filipino domestic helper has become a sort of status symbol. As their salary expectations are generally higher than those of other migrant workers in the market, they are regarded as occupying the top tier in the domestic service hierarchy. In other words, having a Filipino working in the home sends the message to the outside world that "we are doing very well indeed."

### State-supported training and strict supervision

The Philippine government treats this matter entirely as a matter of state policy. Women undergo a rigorous preparation process before even leaving the country, completing certified programs at technical training institutions. They practically memorize the legal framework, social norms, and cultural structure of their destination country during special pre-departure seminars. In other words, they arrive having fully adapted to the country.

### A versatile workforce capable of handling any task

They are generally capable of single-handedly managing the entire running of a household. They are competent in every area, from cooking and cleaning to laundry and ironing, shopping, and even caring for children or the elderly. As they are able to adapt very quickly to different cultures and lifestyles, they are always among the top choices in the international labor market.

## In which sectors do Filipino women work abroad?

When we take a quick look at the global labor market map of Filipino migrant women, we can clearly see the reflections of those classic social roles we are all familiar with. However, this is not simply a matter of finding an ordinary job; it has evolved into a massive, multi-billion-dollar industry in its own right.

### The domestic services and care sector (largest share)

This is precisely where Filipino women have become something of a global brand and made their name most widely known. Nearly a quarter of the women who leave their country — that is, the overwhelming majority of those choosing more basic sectors of work — earn their living through domestic services.

What we refer to as domestic work encompasses every conceivable task, from cleaning to cooking, and from laundry to ironing, and they shoulder this burden entirely on their own. Childcare or nannying, however, is the area most sought after by families. This is because parents want their children to be exposed to English from a very young age and to pick up the language naturally, without any difficulty. On the other hand, there is an ageing global population; particularly in Europe and other developed countries, they provide care for the elderly and the sick, either in private homes or in specialised care centres, to meet this relentless demand. The Philippine government takes this work so seriously that it has officially started referring to these women as **"supermaids"** to make them appear more professional, and subjects them to a rigorous training programme.

### Healthcare Sector

The Philippines has become a country that not only provides unskilled labour but also, in the truest sense of the word, trains qualified healthcare personnel for the world. Among women leaving the country as professional or qualified specialists, **nurses are by far the most numerous.** You are most likely to encounter them in hospitals in countries such as the United States, Canada, and the United Kingdom. Of course, it is not just nursing; they also work tirelessly behind the scenes in professional healthcare services, in various departments of clinics or hospitals, such as midwifery and healthcare administration.

### The entertainment and arts sector

This situation is more prevalent in East Asia, particularly in countries such as Japan. Singers, dancers, and those in the show business world fall into this category. These women, referred to as "Overseas Performing Artists," may occasionally work in hostess bars in these regions. On the other hand, Filipino jazz vocalists, musicians, and models have always held a very stylish and prestigious position in the luxury venues of Asia's major metropolises.

### The service, retail, and tourism sectors

Young Filipino women are also prominent in this field, thanks to their famous warm smiles and, of course, their language skills. They can be found working as shop assistants and sales representatives in large shopping centers or the shops of well-known brands. Similarly, in the hospitality and tourism sector, they can be found everywhere from reception to housekeeping, from waitstaff to tour guides, and even as cabin crew on international airlines. In addition to these, they also take on office and secretarial roles, such as back-office work, data entry, or directly serving as executive assistants to senior management.

### Manufacturing and industrial sector

There is also a significant female workforce toiling as blue-collar workers on the factory floor. They work as production workers on assembly lines in factories producing textiles, ready-to-wear clothing, food packaging, and, most notably, electronic components. In smaller businesses, they work independently in more specialized, labor-intensive trades such as tailoring, which require great care and attention to detail.

### Other areas of expertise and additional work

The talents of Filipino women do not end there. Some women work as massage therapists in the spa centers of luxury hotels — either alongside their main job, such as childcare, to earn some extra pocket money, or entirely independently. They also make excellent private tutors in people's homes, helping children practice English or study outside school hours. Finally, they have carved out a niche for themselves in the world of technology, working in call centers or within the vast IT and communications sector, where business processes are managed externally.

## The legal exit process and documentation for Filipino women

A Filipino woman going abroad to work is certainly not a simple migration story where she simply decides on her own, packs her bags, and flies off. On the contrary, there is a highly institutionalized state policy at play, backed by a vast state apparatus, in which every step is monitored down to the last detail. Indeed, the Philippines is recognized worldwide as a **"model country"** when it comes to managing labor migration and protecting its own people. Every detail, from the preparations before departure right through to reintegration upon return, is closely monitored by the bureaucracy.

In other words, this is not merely a matter of dry bureaucratic formalities on paper; it is a serious, professionalized process — sometimes lasting as long as **14 weeks** — that places the woman's health, professional skills, and rights in the host country under state guarantee. Specifically, the legal departure procedures and the critical documents that Filipino women working in the domestic service and care sectors are required to prepare proceed as follows:

### Basic legal framework and intermediary organisations

For a Filipino woman to be able to legally travel abroad, the process must be overseen by the **DMW (Department of Migrant Workers)** — formerly known as the POEA (Philippine Overseas Employment Administration). The process is generally divided into two main phases and requires approval from the POLO (Philippine Overseas Labor Office) even in cases of direct hire.

### Required legal documents

The application file prepared for departure abroad must contain the following documents:

- **Valid passport:** Must be valid for at least 6 months from the planned departure date.
- **Approved employment contract:** This must be a standard contract prepared by the POEA. The contract must include provisions such as salary (a minimum of 400 USD for domestic workers), working hours (at least 12 hours' uninterrupted rest per day), a weekly day off, and free transport.
- **Employer's undertaking:** This is a formal commitment by the employer to protect the employee's rights and to cooperate with the Philippine Embassy.
- **Contingency Plan:** A document outlining how the employer will evacuate or support the worker in the event of a potential disaster, war, or illness.
- **Compulsory insurance:** The worker must be insured against the risks of illness, accident, or death by a company accredited by the POEA.

### Health screenings and tests

In order for a Filipino woman to obtain an exit visa, she must obtain a "health certificate" from a clinic accredited by the **DOH (Department of Health).** These screenings include the following:

- **AIDS/HIV and Hepatitis B tests:** They must prove that they do not carry any infectious diseases.
- **Drug test and chest X-ray:** These assess physical fitness for heavy labor.
- **Psychological test:** This assesses mental preparedness for loneliness and challenging working conditions abroad.
- **Physical standards:** Data such as height and weight are recorded and form part of the application dossier submitted to the employer.

### Compulsory training courses and seminars

Filipino women are required to complete a series of training programs and obtain a certificate before traveling abroad. The Philippine government classifies women working in the domestic service and care sectors in particular as a **"vulnerable"** group and implements compulsory training programs to protect them:

#### Vocational skills and orientation training

- **TESDA (Technical Education and Skills Development):** Women attend TESDA-approved courses to obtain a professional certificate (NC II) demonstrating their technical competence in domestic services or care work. These courses aim to professionalize the work carried out, thereby strengthening women's position in the workplace.
- **PEOS (Pre-Employment Orientation):** Provided during the job-seeking phase, this training offers comprehensive information across eight modules on legal rights, contract terms, and ways to protect oneself against illegal recruitment practices.
- **PDOS (Pre-departure Orientation):** This one-day seminar, which is compulsory for every worker whose departure has been confirmed, covers topics such as the laws, customs, travel procedures, and financial management of the destination country.
- **CPDEP (Comprehensive Pre-departure Training Programme):** Designed specifically for domestic workers, this program combines language training for the destination country with courses on cultural adaptation and stress management.

#### Values education and the "National Hero" mission

Filipino women do not merely acquire technical knowledge during the training process; they are also reminded that they are **"Bagong Bayani" (New National Heroes).** In the "values formation" section of the training, women are instilled with a sense of their financial responsibilities towards their families, their obligation to send money regularly, and the fact that their behavior abroad represents the Philippines' national image. This creates a psychological foundation that ensures they are perceived as "loyal and disciplined" by employers.

### Final stage: OEC (Overseas Employment Certificate)

Once all documents have been finalized, medical reports approved, and the seminars completed, the woman is issued an **OEC (Overseas Employment Certificate).** This document is the final "exit permit" that enables a Filipino worker to pass through the worker support desk at the airport and board the flight legally. A Filipino woman without an OEC will not be permitted to leave the country, even if she holds a work visa.

## Which countries do Filipino women prefer most?

Filipino migrant women are spread across more than 200 countries and regions worldwide. However, the countries they most frequently choose and where they are most concentrated, in terms of both economic opportunities and ultimate settlement goals, are listed in the following categories:

### Countries with the highest employment and concentration

The regions with the highest numbers of Filipino female workers are the Middle East, Southeast Asia, and East Asia. The vast majority of female migrants work in countries on the Asian continent.

- **Saudi Arabia:** It has been the second most popular destination country for many years and ranks first, hosting a significant proportion of Filipino workers.
- **United Arab Emirates (UAE):** This is a major destination for some Filipino women.
- **Kuwait and Qatar:** These are other highly sought-after countries in the Middle East.
- **Hong Kong and Singapore:** These are among the key hubs, particularly for women working in domestic services and childcare.

### "Dream" and most sought-after countries

Among Filipino women, the migration process is generally based on a hierarchy. While the Middle East and other Asian countries are typically destinations chosen for higher earnings, **the US and Canada rank at the top as "dream" or "ideal" countries.**

- **The US:** This is the number one destination of choice for Filipino migrants. It is also the most popular destination for migration through marriage.
- **Canada:** This is the second most popular destination after the US. Approximately 3 million Filipinos live permanently in Canada.
- **European Countries:** Italy, Spain, and the United Kingdom are the most popular destinations in Europe and are seen as symbols of high status.

### Countries used as "stepping stones"

Many Filipino women view other countries as "stepping stones" on their way to their ultimate destination.

- **Hong Kong:** This is one of the most frequently used transit points, due to the belief that it is easier to travel on to Canada from there.
- **Turkey:** Turkey is increasingly being preferred over the Middle Eastern countries (Kuwait, UAE, and Qatar) due to their harsh working conditions and low wages, as it offers greater freedom and better conditions. However, for most women, Turkey is also regarded as a temporary stopover on the way to Canada or Europe.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is "deskilling" or "brain drain"?**
It refers to the situation in the Philippines where women in professional occupations, such as teaching or nursing, are employed outside their fields of expertise — for example, as domestic workers or carers — abroad in order to earn a higher income.

**What is the most distinctive feature of Filipino women's childcare services?**
It is the compassion they show towards the children in their care, treating them "as if they were their own," and the strong emotional bond they form with them.

**What is "TNT" (Tago ng Tago)?**
This term, which means "always hidden" in Tagalog, refers to undocumented migrants who remain in the country despite their visas having expired.

**Where do migrant women spend the money they earn first and foremost?**
They send a large proportion of their earnings back to their families in the Philippines as remittances to fund their children's education and support the family's livelihood.

**How long does the migration process take for Filipino women?**
The legal process, including training, health screenings, and document approvals, can take approximately 14 weeks.

**What is "transnational motherhood" (distant mothering)?**
It refers to women leaving their children in the Philippines and attempting to fulfill their maternal responsibilities from a distance by working thousands of kilometers away to earn the money needed to care for them.

**Why has the Philippine government awarded domestic workers the title of "super maids"?**
It has used this title with the aim of professionalizing these workers, adding value to them, and protecting them by emphasizing that they have been trained to a higher standard.

**What is the marital status profile of migrant Filipino women?**
The vast majority are married and have children; however, there are also those who are single or who use migration as a "means of divorce" to escape unhappy marriages.

**What is the greatest psychological problem faced by Filipino migrant women?**
Loneliness and homesickness caused by long-term separations, particularly the distress arising from missing the children they have left behind.
