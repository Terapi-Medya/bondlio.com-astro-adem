## Who is a Filipino nanny, and why are they so often preferred?

**The Philippines** has been sending domestic care workers to many countries for decades. Plenty of Filipino nannies have spent years working in cities such as Hong Kong, Singapore, and Dubai, and a good number of those who come to Turkey arrive with that experience.

Families choose them for three main reasons:

* **English:** Most candidates speak English comfortably in daily life. If you want your child to hear a second language naturally from an early age, this is usually the biggest reason.
* **Professional habits:** Many candidates have worked in domestic care as a profession for years, and they have a clear approach to household routines, schedules, and the division of tasks.
* **Calm communication style:** They come from a culture that emphasizes respect for elders and for the host family. Of course this is a generalization; every person is an individual. But what we often hear in interviews is that they are people who find solutions without raising their voice.

## How does a Filipino nanny come to Turkey? How does the legal process work?

Legally employing a foreign nanny in Turkey is possible. For that, a work permit must be obtained through the Ministry of Labor and Social Security, and the employment must be reported to SGK, the Social Security Institution. Undeclared employment carries serious penalties for both the family and the nanny, and we strongly advise against taking that risk.

### The process roughly runs as follows (average 8–10 weeks)

1. **Needs analysis (week 1):** The child's age, household routine, live-in or live-out preference, and language expectations are discussed.
2. **Candidate presentation and video interview (weeks 1–3):** You meet suitable candidates online.
3. **Selection and pre-contract (weeks 2–3):** Both sides review the terms.
4. **Work permit application (weeks 3–7):** Documents are submitted to the Ministry and followed up.
5. **Visa and flight (weeks 7–8):** Timing varies with processing times.
6. **Welcome and orientation (weeks 8–9):** The process runs in a fully controlled way.
7. **Official start and SGK registration (around week 10):** Official procedures are followed up.

**Documents usually required (contact us for the current list):**

* *From the family:* ID, proof of income, the child's population registry record, criminal record certificate.
* *From the candidate:* Valid passport, health report, criminal record certificate, experience and reference documents, education and first aid certificates.

The income eligibility requirement, fees, and contribution rates may change during the year.

## Pros and cons of a Filipino nanny

Most pages only describe the advantages. We write both, because surprises discovered later can cause problems for both the family and the nanny.

**Advantages**

* Natural, intensive use of English in daily life
* The chance to find an experienced candidate who has done this work for years
* Generally an orderly, planned way of working
* Flexibility for the family in a live-in arrangement

**Disadvantages and things to watch**

* **Cost:** Can be noticeably higher than a Turkish nanny.
* **Turkish:** In the first months, communication will be largely in English. There may be difficulty with the child's doctor, school, or grandparents.
* **Process:** Bringing someone from abroad takes several weeks. If you need someone to start "tomorrow," you need a candidate who is already here.
* **Cultural differences:** Food, days off, and religious and social habits may differ. It is much easier to talk about these at the start than later.
* **A family far away:** Most nannies send the bulk of their salary to their family and miss their home country. Regular payment and a humane approach matter a great deal.
* **Attachment:** A child can become very attached to the nanny. That is natural, but as a parent it is very important that you spend quality time with your child.

## Filipino baby nanny

For families with a baby aged 0–3, the first question is usually: "Are they experienced with babies?" The answer depends on the candidate, so look at the following:

* How many babies have they cared for? Do they have experience with newborns?
* How experienced are they with solid foods, bottles, and sleep routines?
* Have they had first aid and infant CPR training?
* If you need nighttime care, are they suitable for it?

Real experience in baby care is a more important criterion than the candidate's age or how many countries they have worked in. Always call the references.

## Looking for a Filipino nanny: 10 questions for the interview

1. How many years have you worked as a nanny, and in which countries?
2. Why did you leave your last job?
3. What responsibilities have you taken on most with this age group?
4. What do you do first when a child has a fever or falls?
5. How do you handle it when a child refuses to eat?
6. Could you briefly describe a typical daily routine?
7. How long do you plan to stay in Turkey?
8. Is a live-in arrangement right for you, and how do you plan your days off?
9. Can you give references from previous employers?
10. Do you have any questions for us?

**Pay attention to the last question.** Good candidates usually ask about the child's routine, the house rules, and the days-off arrangement.

**Warning signs:** avoiding giving references, constantly changing jobs after short periods, contradictory accounts, reluctance to provide documents.

Although it is often assumed that Filipino women are placed in Turkey only through an agency before they arrive, the process runs through a comprehensive system of professional, cultural, and legal training that the Philippine government makes mandatory.

#### Vocational and technical education (TESDA)

Candidates who will work in childcare, cleaning, or household help attend TESDA courses and receive an **NC II certificate**, showing they have acquired the skills needed, from baby care to household management.

#### Pre-Employment Orientation (PEOS)

In the PEOS seminar, candidates learn about the process of leaving the country, workers' rights, what to watch for in a contract, and the risks posed by fake agents.

#### Pre-Departure Orientation (PDOS)

The PDOS seminar covers Turkey's laws, culture, customs, and daily life, helping candidates adapt to their new country and understand the responsibility they carry.

#### Comprehensive Pre-Departure Education Program (CPDEP)

CPDEP covers basic Turkish, culture shock, and coping with homesickness. The aim is to make candidates better prepared for life and working conditions in Turkey.

#### Financial literacy training

Candidates learn how to manage their earnings safely, send money to their families with low fees, save, and build a financial plan for the future.

#### Post-Arrival Orientation (PAOS)

After arrival in Turkey, the PAOS seminar covers current laws, the limits of the work permit, and the rights of both worker and employer. This way the process continues in an official and informed way in Turkey as well.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a Filipino nanny?

A domestic care professional who is a citizen of the Philippines and works in child or baby care. In Turkey, they are mostly preferred for their English and for the live-in working model.

### How much does a Filipino nanny earn?

For candidates brought from abroad, a live-in salary is roughly $1,000 a month. For experienced candidates already in Turkey, it is roughly $1,400–2,000. The amount varies with experience, the working arrangement, and the family's expectations.

### Is it legal to employ a Filipino nanny in Turkey?

Yes, they can be employed legally by obtaining a work permit and reporting to SGK. Employing without a permit leads to serious penalties.

### How long does it take for a Filipino nanny to come to Turkey?

On average 8–10 weeks. The speed of document completion and the Ministry's processing time affect the result. For candidates already in Turkey, this time can be much shorter.

### Do Filipino nannies learn Turkish?

Most candidates learn everyday commands in the first months and simple conversations in the months after. The pace varies from person to person. If communication in English can continue within the family, this is not a problem.

### Do Filipino nannies do baby care?

Many do, but experience varies. For baby care, always ask about the candidate's experience with babies and check their references.

### Do they work live-in or live-out?

Both are possible. The live-in model is more common in Turkey. Live-out options are generally easier to find among candidates who are already in Turkey.

### Should I choose a Filipino or an Indonesian nanny?

If English is your priority, a Filipino candidate may suit you better. If cultural and religious closeness is your priority, an Indonesian candidate may be more suitable. With either option, what matters is the individual fit of the candidate.

### I'm looking for a Filipino nanny. Where should I start?

First clarify your budget, your live-in or live-out preference, and your child's age. Then proceed through a trustworthy consultancy or directly through candidate interviews. Don't skip the reference check.
